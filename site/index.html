A cost-and-options review of The Mingle Movement’s path to the App Store & Google Play — and why the honest number is closer to $6k–$15k than the $50k quote.

Prepared for Luke & Richard Subject The Mingle Movement (live web app + mobile shell) Date 10 Aug 2026

The one thing that changes everything

The quotes Richard received ($50k–$150k) are priced as if the app is being built from scratch. It isn’t. Reading his own technical brief: the product is live in production, the backend is complete (~45 tables, admin console, video calling, a full nurture engine), and the iOS/Android shell is already wired — just not submitted to the stores yet.

What’s left is not a build. It’s a finish-and-ship: package the existing app, activate payments, add the one genuinely hard native piece (a real incoming-call screen), and get through store review. That reframe is worth roughly 5–10× on price. Everything below flows from it.

The decision

Four options, cheapest to most expensive

Ranked by out-of-pocket cost. My recommendation is a blend of the first two — explained after the cards.

OPTION 01

Do it yourself, AI-assisted

$0–$3k+ your time
Cheapest cash outlay Highest time cost

Use Claude Code and AI tooling to do the packaging work yourselves. Because the app is already built, most of what’s left is configuration and integration, not architecture — which is exactly where AI is strongest. Luke, this is the “can we just do it ourselves?” path, and for a good chunk of the list the answer is genuinely yes.

AI handles well

  • Store packaging, permission strings, config files
  • Store listings, screenshots, descriptions
  • Wiring & testing in-app purchases
  • Deep-link setup

Where DIY stalls

  • The native call screen (CallKit/ConnectionService) — specialist territory
  • Apple/Google review rejections for a dating app
  • Signing certificates & store console gotchas

Hard costs: Apple Developer $99/yr, Google Play $25 once, AI tooling ~$20–200/mo, plus a Mac for iOS builds.

Verdict: Realistically covers 70–80% of the remaining work for near-zero cash. The call-screen piece and the store-review back-and-forth are where a solo/AI effort tends to grind for weeks. Great for control and learning; risky as the only plan given an October revenue target.
OPTION 02

Hire one vetted Capacitor specialist

$6k–$18kfixed scope
Recommended Best value

Hire a single freelance developer who specialises in Capacitor / Ionic native builds, on a fixed-scope, milestone engagement — not open-ended hourly. Point them at the finish-and-ship list, and especially the hard native call screen. The existing backend stays untouched; the brief confirms it’s “client-agnostic.”

  • Milestone 1 — both apps built, signed, and submitted with listings & permissions
  • Milestone 2 — in-app purchases live and validated end-to-end
  • Milestone 3 — incoming calls ring on the lock screen (the single biggest win)
  • Milestone 4 — through Apple & Google review, live in both stores
Verdict: The sweet spot. Roughly 1/3 to 1/8 the cost of the quotes, de-risks the one genuinely hard piece, and keeps everything that already works. Pay per milestone so Richard only pays for results that land. This is what I’d do.
OPTION 03

Boutique app agency (hybrid polish)

$25k–$55kmanaged team
Premium for convenience

A small studio does the finish-and-ship and rebuilds the highest-traffic screens (Browse + the call experience) natively for polish and performance, keeping the backend. You’re paying for a managed team, a warranty, project management, and a maintenance relationship — not for missing product.

Verdict: Worth it only if Richard wants a hands-off, accountable team and ongoing support, and is comfortable spending 3–5× Option 2 to get it. At 680 members pre-launch, hard to justify yet — but a reasonable “phase 2” once revenue is real.

OPTION 04

Full native rewrite

$80k–$150k+rebuild the client
Avoid — this is what the quotes priced

Rebuild the entire app as pure native (Swift + Kotlin) against the same backend. This is almost certainly what generated the $50k–$150k figures — it prices a whole client from zero.

Verdict: Not warranted. The product, backend, admin, and shell already exist and are reusable. A rewrite throws away working software to chase native performance a pre-launch product doesn’t need yet. Revisit only at serious scale (tens of thousands of active users), if ever.

Protect the downside

How to hire without getting burned

  • Ask the filter question: “Walk me through how you’d make an incoming call ring on the lock screen with CallKit and ConnectionService.” A confident, specific answer separates specialists from generalists. Everything else on the list is routine.
  • Start with a small paid trial — e.g. get the app building and submitted (Milestone 1) before committing to the whole scope.
  • Fixed price per milestone, each tied to something demonstrable (“call rings on my locked iPhone”), not hours logged.
  • Richard owns every account — Apple Developer, Google Play, Supabase, domain. The developer gets invited access, never the keys. This is the mistake that traps founders.
  • Use the NDA he already has, and keep the detailed technical brief for shortlisted candidates only — as he’s already planned.

The business lens

Two things that gate the October plan

These aren’t development problems — they’re on the critical path to the 1,000-subscriber, $30k/month projection, and they’re easy to under-prioritise.

Blocker 1 — Merchant / payment approval Card payments are still “stubbed, pending merchant approval,” and native billing needs App Store / Play approval. No payments = no $30k/month, no matter how good the app is. This is a business/banking task with its own lead time — start it this week, in parallel with any development. Don’t let it be discovered in October.
Blocker 2 — Dating-app store review Apple and Google scrutinise dating apps hard (user-generated content rules): they require a real age gate, blocking, reporting, and a moderation disclosure. The good news — Richard’s brief shows he already has all of these. The task is making them obvious to a reviewer, or expect a first-round rejection and a week’s delay. Budget for one or two review rounds.
The payback math At the projected 1,000 subscribers × ~$30, that’s ~$30k/month. An Option-2 finish-and-ship (~$10k) pays for itself in under two weeks of projected revenue. Even so, don’t overpay: the $50k quote isn’t “affordable because revenue is coming” — it’s the wrong scope. Remember Apple/Google take 15–30% of in-app subscription revenue; price the tiers and the projection with that haircut baked in.

Bottom line

What I’d actually do

Blend Options 1 and 2. Do the easy 70% yourselves, AI-assisted; pay a specialist for the hard 30%.

Realistic all-in: ~$6k–$15k and 3–6 weeks to live in both stores — against a $50k quote for work that isn’t needed.

  1. Start merchant/payment approval this week. It’s the longest-lead item and it gates revenue, not code. (Richard, in parallel with everything else.)
  2. Do the store-packaging groundwork yourselves, AI-assisted. Listings, screenshots, permissions, deep links, IAP wiring. Cheap, and it builds real understanding of your own product.
  3. Hire one vetted Capacitor specialist on fixed milestones for the native call screen, IAP validation, and final store submission. Use the filter question. Budget $6k–$12k; target Eastern Europe or a vetted marketplace for the best value/risk balance, or local AU if timezone and trust matter more than price.
  4. Make moderation, blocking, reporting and the age gate obvious to reviewers, then submit. Expect one review round; keep going.
  5. Ship, then revisit Option 3 later — only once revenue is real and scale demands native polish. Not before.
