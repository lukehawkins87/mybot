# SOP: Editing Live Event & Intervention Videos

**Company:** Personal Development Training
**Owner:** Luke Hawkins
**Applies to:** All editors cutting live speaking, coaching demonstrations, and audience intervention footage
**Last updated:** 2026-07-05

---

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines how to edit live event footage (keynotes, live coaching interventions, Q&A, hot seats) into content that:

1. **Engages the viewer** from the first seconds and holds them.
2. **Makes the concept understandable** — the viewer always knows what topic is being discussed and why it matters.
3. **Flows like a real conversation** — cuts are invisible; interventions feel natural, not stitched together.
4. **Presents Luke and the coaching at its best** — the questions land, the insights are clear, and the change created in the room is visible on screen.
5. **Shows social proof** — audience reaction, agreement, laughter, emotion, and applause are used deliberately to signal that what's being said is valuable.

Every editing decision should be tested against these five goals. If a cut doesn't serve at least one of them, it doesn't belong in the video.

---

## 2. Core Editing Principles

### 2.1 Context before content (non-negotiable)

**Never open a video mid-statement with no setup.** Before any teaching point or intervention begins, the viewer must know enough to understand what concept or topic is being discussed.

Acceptable ways to establish context (use one or more):

- **A setup clip** — a moment where Luke frames the topic ("Let's talk about why people self-sabotage…") placed before the main content, even if it happened at a different point in the event.
- **A title card / text overlay** — 2–4 seconds, e.g. *"Live intervention: letting go of the fear of judgment."*
- **A cold-open hook + reset** — a short, high-impact moment (max 10–15 seconds) used as a teaser, followed by a clear context beat before the full segment plays.
- **A participant's question** — if the audience member's question frames the topic clearly, open on the question, not the answer.

**Test:** Show the first 20 seconds to someone who wasn't at the event. If they can't say what the video is about, re-cut the opening.

### 2.2 The conversation must flow

Interventions and Q&A must feel like one continuous, natural conversation:

- **Cut on action and on breath.** Cut during a gesture, a head turn, or the natural pause between sentences — never mid-word or mid-gesture unless covered by a cutaway.
- **Hide jump cuts** with audience reaction shots, wide shots, or the participant's face. If you must jump cut on the same angle, keep it only where the energy justifies it.
- **Preserve conversational rhythm.** Keep the participant's real hesitations and emotional pauses when they show processing or breakthrough. Trim dead air, filler, and repeated false starts that don't add meaning.
- **Question → space → answer.** When Luke asks a powerful question, leave a beat of silence after it. Don't rush to the answer — the pause is where the viewer feels the weight of the question.
- **Never cut away from a breakthrough moment.** Tears, realizations, long silences before an honest answer: hold the shot. This is the product.

### 2.3 Luke must look good — and the coaching must look effective

- **Select the strongest takes/angles.** Prefer shots where Luke is composed, energised, and connected to the person or audience. Avoid frames catching mid-blink, awkward mouth positions, or unflattering angles as cut-in points.
- **Show the questions, not just the answers.** The value of the coaching is in the quality of the questions. Keep Luke on camera (or cut to him) when he asks the key question so the viewer associates the insight with him.
- **Show cause and effect.** Structure intervention edits so the viewer sees: Luke says/asks something → the participant visibly shifts (face, posture, tears, laughter, a lighter answer). That before/after arc is the proof the coaching works — protect it in the edit.
- **Cut hedging and rambling.** If a statement can be tightened without changing its meaning, tighten it. Luke should sound clear, confident, and precise on screen.
- **Never make the participant look bad.** They must always be treated with dignity — their vulnerability is courage, not content to be exploited. If a moment could embarrass them, cut it or reframe it.

### 2.4 Show audience approval

Wherever it genuinely occurred, show the room responding:

- Nods, smiles, leaning in, note-taking during teaching points.
- Laughter after humour — cut to the laughing faces, not just the audio.
- Applause after a breakthrough or strong close — let it breathe for 2–3 seconds before cutting.
- Emotional reactions (audience members moved to tears during an intervention) — these are the most powerful social proof available. Use them.

**Rules:**
- Reaction shots must be plausible in timing — a reaction cut should match the moment it responds to. Never use a reaction that reads as fake or misplaced; one false-feeling reaction undermines the credibility of the whole video.
- Reactions double as cut-hiders (see 2.2) — plan them to do both jobs at once.

## 3. Pre-Edit Workflow

1. **Ingest and back up** all camera angles and audio before touching an edit. Verify sync sources (timecode or clap/spike).
2. **Sync multicam** and create a synced sequence per segment.
3. **Get or generate a transcript** of the full session. Edit from the transcript first — it's the fastest way to find the story, the best questions, and the quotable lines.
4. **Log selects.** Mark:
   - The strongest questions Luke asks
   - Breakthrough/shift moments (the emotional peaks)
   - Audience reaction moments (bank these — you'll need more than you think)
   - Context/framing statements usable as openings
   - Quotable one-liners for shorts
5. **Identify the arc** of each intervention: setup → problem surfaced → key question → resistance/processing → shift → resolution/landing. The edit should make this arc obvious.

---

## 4. Edit Workflow

1. **Radio edit first.** Build the story with audio only from the transcript selects. If it works with eyes closed, it will work on screen.
2. **Add picture.** Choose angles per beat:
   - Key questions and teaching statements → Luke's camera
   - Emotional answers and shifts → participant close-up
   - Energy, scale, and transitions → wide shot
   - Cut-hiding and social proof → audience reactions
3. **Build the opening** per section 2.1 (context first, hook allowed, no orphan statements).
4. **Pacing pass.** Target: no more than ~30 seconds on a single static angle during conversation; slower cutting during emotional moments, faster during high-energy teaching. Trim every pause that doesn't carry meaning; keep every pause that does.
5. **Music and sound design** (see section 5).
6. **Graphics pass** (see section 6).
7. **Colour and finishing** (see section 7).

---

## 5. Audio Standards

Audio quality is more important than picture quality — viewers forgive soft images, never bad sound.

- **Dialogue first.** Use the lav/handheld mic feed, not camera audio. Denoise and de-reverb as needed, but keep it natural.
- **Loudness:** normalise dialogue to approximately −16 LUFS (stereo) for social/web; keep true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.
- **Music:** subtle underscore may be used to support emotional moments and openings/closings. Music must never compete with speech (duck at least 15–20 dB under dialogue). No music during raw breakthrough moments — silence is stronger.
- **Room tone:** fill any gaps with room tone so cuts are inaudible. An audible audio jump breaks the "natural conversation" illusion instantly.
- **Audience audio:** keep genuine laughter/applause from the room mics; never add canned reactions.

---

## 6. Graphics, Captions & Text

- **Burned-in captions on all social cuts.** Most viewers start with sound off. Captions must be accurate — clean up filler words in captions to match the tightened audio.
- **Context title card** at or near the top when the setup clip alone isn't enough (see 2.1).
- **Lower third** identifying Luke ("Luke Hawkins — [role/company]") within the first 30 seconds of any video where a new viewer might not know who he is.
- **Key-point callouts:** when Luke says a core line, a short on-screen text emphasis is allowed — sparingly (max 2–3 per long-form video).
- Keep all graphics on brand (fonts, colours, logo placement) and inside title-safe margins for every target platform.

---

## 7. Picture & Colour

- Match colour and exposure across all cameras first, then apply the brand grade.
- Grade for warm, energetic, professional — skin tones accurate, stage lighting controlled, no crushed blacks hiding audience reactions.
- Reframe/punch-in is allowed (up to ~20% on 4K sources) to improve composition or create an extra "angle" for hiding cuts.
- Stabilise any distracting handheld movement.

## 8. Deliverables & Formats

For each event segment, the standard package is:

| Deliverable | Length | Format | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-form edit | 8–20 min | 16:9, YouTube | Full intervention or teaching block with complete arc |
| Mid-form clip | 2–5 min | 16:9 and 9:16 | One complete idea or one complete intervention beat |
| Shorts/Reels | 30–60 sec | 9:16 | Hook in first 2 seconds; context in first 5; one idea only; captions always |
| Quote clips | 10–20 sec | 9:16 / 1:1 | Single powerful line + reaction; for ads and stories |

Every short must still obey the context rule (2.1) — a hook line is fine, but within the first ~5 seconds the viewer must know the topic.

---

## 9. Quality Control Checklist (before delivery)

- [ ] First 20 seconds: topic is clear to a cold viewer (context rule)
- [ ] No cut is audible or visually jarring; conversation feels continuous
- [ ] Every key question Luke asks is on his camera or clearly attributed to him
- [ ] At least one visible cause-and-effect arc: question → shift in the participant
- [ ] Audience approval shown where it genuinely occurred; no misplaced reactions
- [ ] Participant is portrayed with dignity throughout
- [ ] Dialogue at target loudness; music ducked; no audio jumps; room tone filled
- [ ] Captions accurate and synced (all vertical/social deliverables)
- [ ] Lower third and brand graphics correct; safe margins respected
- [ ] Colour matched across angles; brand grade applied
- [ ] Correct export specs per platform; filenames follow convention below

## 10. File Naming & Delivery

`YYYY-MM-DD_EventName_SegmentTopic_Format_vX`
Example: `2026-06-14_BreakthroughLive_FearOfJudgment_9x16_v2.mp4`

Deliver to the agreed shared drive folder for the event, with the project file and a selects bin archived alongside the exports.

---

## 11. Consent & Ethics

- Only publish intervention footage of participants who have signed a release for the event.
- If a participant shares something highly sensitive, flag it to Luke before including it in any public cut — even with a release on file.
- When in doubt about whether a moment honours the participant, escalate rather than publish.
