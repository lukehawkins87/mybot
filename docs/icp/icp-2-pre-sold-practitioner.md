# Audience Profile & Messaging Brief
## ICP 2 — The Pre-Sold Practitioner

*A working guide for writing emails, ads and marketing content that speaks directly to this audience.*

---

### Who they are
Someone who is **already a coach, therapist or healer.** They have done the training — often a lot of it (NLP, hypnotherapy, breathwork, energy work, counselling). They are genuinely good at the actual work and they believe in it deeply.

And they are still under-earning. They trade hours for dollars, undercharge, and watch less-skilled people out-earn them. The gap was never their ability — it is that no one ever handed them a finished program to sell, a way to get clients, and the confidence to charge what they are worth.

They arrive already believing. They do not need convincing that this works. They need a competent, proven system to turn their gift into a real income — and a certification that makes it official.

**In one line:** the skilled practitioner who is brilliant at the work and finally ready to be paid like it.

### What they really want
- **A finished program to sell** — something proven they can run with clients, so they stop inventing it from scratch.
- **A way to actually get clients** — marketing and simple systems. This is the gap almost every one of them admits to.
- **Certification and legitimacy** — a credential (and insurance) that turns "what I do" into a recognised profession.
- **Freedom and flexibility** — the laptop-by-the-ocean life, travel funded by the work, escape from a draining day job.
- **To be seen** — to step out as the expert they already are, and to prove the doubters wrong.

### What holds them back
- **They undercharge** and know it — but do not know how to change it.
- **Marketing is their blind spot** — "that's my gap" is almost a direct quote for this group.
- Fear of being visible / putting themselves out there.
- A lifetime of over-giving — being everyone's free support.

### How they talk (use their language)
- "Marketing is my gap."
- "I know I undercharge."
- "I've done all the training — I just can't seem to make it pay."
- "I want an actual program I can deliver, not more theory."
- "I need systems."
- "I'm more valuable than this."
- "I don't want to feel salesy or sleazy about it."

### Messaging angles that land
- **"You don't need more training. You need the system that finally makes your gifts pay."**
- **"You've done the deep work on yourself. Now get paid to do it for others."**
- **"The healer's problem was never the healing. It was the marketing."**
- **"Stop giving away what you're meant to charge for."**
- **"A finished program to sell — you don't have to build it from scratch."**
- **"For coaches who are already good, and finally ready to be paid like it."**

**Headline / subject-line starters**
- You're already a coach. You don't need convincing — you need a way.
- "Marketing is my gap." Sound familiar?
- From soul-draining day job to a practice you actually love.
- You're worth far more than you're charging. Let's fix that.
- One client can make the whole thing back. The rest is freedom.
- Certified, insured, and finally sellable.

**Email angle ideas**
1. The gap no coach admits to — open on the universal confession ("I undercharge / marketing's my gap"), normalise it, then point to the fix.
2. One client makes it back — the simple maths of a single premium client versus the cost of getting started.
3. You already know it works — for warm audiences: they have seen the method; invite them to make it official.

### Tone & style
- **Respect their skill.** Never teach them the basics or talk down — they are already good, and being treated like a beginner offends them.
- Position the offer as the **missing pieces**: the program to sell, the marketing, the certification — not "learn to coach."
- Honour the "it was never about the money, but I deserve to be paid" tension — align money with alignment and impact.
- Marketing must never feel gross or high-pressure — they are allergic to sleazy tactics.
