The Science

Why Willpower Alone Can't Beat Addiction

To understand why NTT works, you first need to understand the brain. There are three distinct "brains" operating inside you simultaneously — and only one of them is in charge when addiction strikes.

Brain 1 — Conscious

Prefrontal Cortex
("Thinking Brain")

  • Planning & decisions
  • Short-term memory
  • Free will & choices
  • Problem solving
Brain 2 — Subconscious

Limbic Brain
("Emotional Brain")

  • Emotions (the engine of behaviour)
  • Habits & patterns
  • Beliefs & values
  • Long-term memory
  • Emotional memories
Brain 3 — Survival

Brainstem
("Reptilian Brain")

  • Survival instincts
  • Fight / Flight / Freeze
  • Automatic body functions
  • Instinctual reactions
10%

The conscious mind controls just 10% of your emotions and behaviours

90%

The subconscious + brainstem run 90% — automatically, below awareness

What this means for addiction: When someone reaches for a drink, a drug, or a compulsive behaviour, it is not the conscious "thinking brain" making that choice. It is the subconscious emotional brain running an automatic pattern — one that was programmed by past emotional experiences. Telling someone to "just stop" targets the 10%. NTT targets the 90%.

Step by Step

The NTT Personal Breakthrough Session for Addiction

This is the complete clinical process used to remove addiction at its root. Each phase builds on the last. Here is what happens and why it works.

1

Establish Rapport & Take a Detailed Personal History

~60–90 minutes

Before any technique is applied, the practitioner builds trust and gathers a complete map of the client's life — not just the addiction, but the events, emotions, and meanings behind it.

  • Why are you here? (open, exploratory)
  • How would you describe the problem?
  • What is the deepest, most negative meaning your brain makes about you from this?
  • When did it start? What happened first?
  • Every significant emotional event (SEE) along the timeline is mapped and noted — including the emotions felt and the beliefs formed at each event.
Why it works: The brain does not create addiction randomly. It creates it in response to a chain of emotionally significant events. By mapping the full timeline, the practitioner identifies the root cause — not just the most recent trigger. Research in neuroscience confirms that long-term memory is stored in the limbic system as emotional snapshots; finding and working with those snapshots is what makes lasting change possible.
2

Map the 9 Emotional Needs Across the Timeline

~15–20 minutes

For each of the 9 core emotional needs, the practitioner asks whether any events (not yet mentioned) triggered the client to feel that need go unmet. For each event, the practitioner records:

  • The age it occurred
  • Which negative emotions are attached (anger, sadness, fear, hurt, guilt, shame)
  • What limiting beliefs or disempowering meanings were formed

Why it works: Addiction is almost always a strategy to meet one or more of these fundamental human needs. Alcohol may provide a sense of belonging; substances may provide the feeling of being accepted. By locating exactly which needs are unmet and which events created the lack, the session can target the precise neural pathways driving the addictive behaviour.
3

Identify Values, Conflicts & Secondary Gain

~20–35 minutes

The practitioner elicits the client's values in the relevant life area (e.g. health, relationships, freedom). For each top value, they identify:

  • What the value means to the client
  • How the client knows when they are experiencing it (their evidence criteria)
  • Whether that criteria is within their control and accessible now — or whether it is a disempowering rule that keeps them from experiencing it
  • Whether any values are in conflict with each other (e.g. "freedom" conflicts with "responsibility")
  • Whether the client secretly fears experiencing a value (e.g. fear of success, fear of true intimacy)

The practitioner also explores secondary gain — the hidden benefit the problem provides (e.g. the addiction provides an excuse to avoid responsibility, or keeps the client connected to a social group).

Why it works: If a person holds unconscious values that conflict — or if their rules for experiencing a value are impossible to meet — the subconscious will always undermine conscious efforts to change. Identifying and resolving these conflicts removes the internal resistance that causes relapse.
4

Release All Major Negative Emotions (The Gestalt Process)

~60–90 minutes

This is the core of the NTT intervention. Using the Negative Emotions Release Script, the practitioner guides the client into a light trance state and works along their personal timeline to release the stored emotional charge from every significant event.

The Gestalt Explained

Think of the timeline as a string of beads. Every bead is a significant emotional event — all linked together, all holding the same emotion (e.g. anger). The first bead is the root event; it contains the most charge. When that first bead is cleared and its positive lessons are extracted, the entire chain releases simultaneously.

The 10-Step Negative Emotions Release Process

  • Step 1 – Agreement: "Have you decided to release [emotion] from your past today, knowing it's no longer serving you?"
  • Step 2 – Find the Root Cause: The client imagines floating up in a bubble above their timeline. They are guided back to the very first event they ever held this emotion — even before birth if necessary — identifying the precise origin point.
  • Step 3 – Rise Above the Event: From the safety of the bubble, the client floats above the event to a "place of no emotion" — above the stars, the galaxy — until the event is a tiny, neutral speck below them.
  • Step 4 – Imagine the Event from Above: From this dissociated, safe vantage point, the client observes the event without being inside it.
  • Step 5 – Float to No Emotion: The client rises to a place of total stillness and peace — completely detached from the emotional charge of the event.
  • Step 6 – Extract the Positive Lessons: "What are the positive lessons your subconscious mind needs you to take from this event in order to release the emotion?" The client receives the learnings naturally.
  • Step 7 – Check Before the Event: The client travels in the bubble to before the event — and checks whether the old emotion is there or gone.
  • Step 8 – Check Inside the Event: The client enters the event through their own eyes and checks whether the emotion is there or gone.
  • Step 9 – Float Back to Now: The client releases the emotion across every event between then and now, all the way back to the present.
  • Step 10 – Test Past & Future: The client recalls a past time they felt the emotion — is it still there? Then imagines a future scenario where it would have arisen — is it still there?

This process is repeated for each of the 6 major negative emotions: Anger → Sadness → Fear → Hurt → Guilt → Shame.

Why it works: The brain cannot distinguish between a vividly imagined experience and a real one. By dissociating from the event (floating above it in a bubble), the client accesses the memory without re-traumatising themselves. Extracting positive lessons is the key neurological mechanism — the subconscious holds onto negative emotions as a protection and warning system. Once the lesson is consciously received, the protection is no longer needed and the emotion releases. This mirrors the neuroscience of memory reconsolidation: each time a memory is recalled and updated, it can be re-stored in a new form. The timeline structure addresses the entire Gestalt chain simultaneously, which is why change can happen rapidly.
5

Eliminate All Limiting Beliefs

~60 minutes

After the emotional charge is cleared, the practitioner works through every limiting belief identified in the history — using the same timeline-based learning process.

  • The first 3–4 beliefs are cleared directly, one at a time
  • For subsequent beliefs, the practitioner checks: "You used to believe [belief] — do you still believe that?" Often the learnings from earlier clearings will have already dissolved them
  • Any belief still present is cleared using the Limiting Belief Script

Common addiction-related limiting beliefs include:

  • "I'm not good enough without it"
  • "I can't cope with stress alone"
  • "I don't deserve to be well"
  • "People only like me when I'm using"
  • "I have no willpower"
  • "This is just who I am"
Why it works: Limiting beliefs are stored as generalised neurological rules in the subconscious mind. They were formed as logical conclusions from emotionally charged events — they made sense at the time. Once the emotional charge on those events is removed (Phase 4), the logical foundation of the belief collapses. The limiting belief script extracts the positive learning that makes the belief unnecessary, allowing the brain to adopt an empowering alternative automatically.
6

Align Values & Clear Values Conflicts

~20 minutes

With emotions and beliefs cleared, the practitioner returns to the client's values hierarchy and:

  • Clears any remaining disempowering criteria or rules around the top 4 values
  • Removes any specific fears attached to values (e.g. fear of truly experiencing love, fear of success)
  • Checks whether the order of the values hierarchy supports the client's goals — and reorders if needed
Why it works: A person's values drive their behaviour automatically. If "freedom" is the top value but is unconsciously associated with the substance ("the only time I feel free is when I drink"), then the value itself will trigger craving. Clearing the emotional associations from values and ensuring the hierarchy supports sobriety makes the client's deepest drivers work for recovery rather than against it.
7

Ecology Check, Future Pacing & Goal Installation

~20 minutes

Before completing the session, the practitioner checks that all changes are complete and ecologically sound:

  • Comprehensive review: "Is there any emotion or limiting thought pattern we have missed?" Anything remaining is cleared before continuing.
  • Evidence test: The client is asked to reproduce the original problem — in most cases they cannot. The compulsive feeling is simply gone.
  • Ecology check: "Is there any reason it would not be okay for this change to continue into the future?" Any conflict is resolved.
  • Future pace: The client mentally projects out 1 year, 5 years, 10 years — observing themselves living free of the addiction. This installs a new neurological pattern in the subconscious future timeline.
  • Goal installation: One or more empowering goals are installed for 1 month, 6 months, and 1 year — giving the brain a compelling positive future to move toward.
Why it works: The subconscious mind moves toward vividly imagined futures. Mentally rehearsing a new identity and new behaviours creates neural pathways for those behaviours before they happen in reality — the same mechanism used by Olympic athletes in mental performance training. Installing goals also activates the brain's reticular activating system (RAS), which begins filtering reality to find evidence of the new identity.
8

The Three Requisites for Lasting Change

Ongoing

At the close of every session, the client is reminded that the session creates the foundation — but these three elements lock the change in permanently:

  • 1. Inner work done: Negative emotions, fears and limiting beliefs cleared; values aligned; goals set. (Completed in session.)
  • 2. Take action: The client commits to specific behavioural changes and boundary-setting in daily life.
  • 3. Focus on what you want: Thoughts and internal images become suggestions to the subconscious mind. Journalling, meditation, and visualisation keep the brain pointed in the right direction.

The practitioner assigns tailored tasks — self-anchoring, journalling, exercise, or other practices — and checks in at 1 week, 2 weeks, and 3 weeks post-session.

Why it works: Neuroplasticity — the brain's ability to rewire itself — requires both a clear emotional state (achieved in session) and repeated new behavioural inputs (achieved through tasking). The session dissolves the old wiring; consistent new actions build the new wiring. Both are essential.

Summary

Why NTT Works Where Other Approaches Struggle

Traditional approaches (willpower, talk therapy, 12-step programmes) primarily engage the conscious mind — the 10% of the brain that is not running the addiction. They address symptoms and coping strategies. They rarely access the subconscious emotional memories that created the problem.
NTT goes directly to the subconscious — the 90%. It locates the precise emotional events on the timeline that programmed the addictive pattern, extracts the positive learnings from those events (giving the subconscious permission to let go), and dissolves the limiting beliefs, negative emotions, and values conflicts that sustain the behaviour. The result is not suppression or management — it is removal at the root.
